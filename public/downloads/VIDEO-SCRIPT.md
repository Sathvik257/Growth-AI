# Three-minute walkthrough script

Shot plan for the three-minute product walkthrough. The MP4 uses disclosed synthetic narration; all campaign results shown are simulated.

## 0:00 - 0:30 | 01 / THE PROBLEM
This is Campus Relay, an AI-assisted prototype for the NxtWave growth challenge. The goal is five hundred final-year engineering student registrations in seven days, with a budget of two thousand rupees. The strategy starts with trusted campus networks. The target student wants a practical AI project they can explain in a placement interview. A clear project preview and a credible invitation should make registration more compelling. Every result shown here is simulated.

## 0:30 - 1:00 | 02 / THE GROWTH MODEL
Three channels provide the base case. Twenty active ambassadors, recruited from sixty prospects, cover forty permitted groups. Four thousand eight hundred unique people reached, at fifteen percent visits and fifty percent conversion, produce three hundred sixty registrations. Three hundred existing campus or club registrants seed referrals: forty percent share with two friends, producing one hundred twenty registrations if both visit and half register. Clubs add forty more. Total: five hundred twenty, with every assumption explicit.

## 1:00 - 1:30 | 03 / STUDENT EXPERIENCE
The student flow collects fictional details and declared study year. It rejects non-final-year entries and duplicate emails, including capitalization changes. A successful registration receives a personal referral link. A second student opening that link is attributed to student referrals. The code must belong to an existing registration. This works in the same browser profile. It is a local prototype, so it does not verify identity or aggregate registrations across devices.

## 1:30 - 2:00 | 04 / CAMPAIGN DESK
The campaign check-in makes the tracker useful for decisions. Enter cumulative unique visits, active ambassadors and spend. These are manually entered simulation inputs. With one hundred eighty registrations on day six, the goal needs three hundred twenty more, or one hundred sixty per day including today. At the base rates, that requires about four thousand two hundred sixty-seven additional people reached. Low activation, weak conversion and an exhausted budget trigger different recommendations.

## 2:00 - 2:30 | 05 / BUDGET AND JUDGMENT
The budget is capped at two thousand rupees: one thousand for actual ambassador expenses, five hundred for necessary club logistics, and five hundred contingency. There is no reward per signup. The conservative scenario lowers conversion rather than quietly improving it: two hundred sixteen campus registrations, forty-eight referrals, and thirty-two from clubs. Total: two hundred ninety-six. The plan therefore tests access and conversion early, and reports the gap honestly if additional reach is unavailable.

## 2:30 - 3:00 | 06 / AI AND LEARNING
Three changes show how the reasoning improved. The first invitation asked for one friend while the math assumed two visits; the copy and model now agree. Generic dashboard advice became a check-in driven by entered measurements. A supposedly conservative scenario had higher referral conversion; that assumption was rejected. Another twenty-four hours would prioritize student interviews, shared storage, email verification and volunteer testing. This is an AI-assisted simulation with synthetic narration, not an executed campaign.
