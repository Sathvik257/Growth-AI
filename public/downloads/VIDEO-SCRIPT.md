# Three-minute walkthrough script

Synthetic narration is included in the MP4. For a personal application, use this shot plan to record the explanation in your own voice. Do not claim simulated registrations as real.

## 0:00 - 0:30 | 01 / THE PROBLEM
This is Campus Relay, an AI-assisted prototype for the NxtWave growth challenge. The goal is five hundred final-year engineering student registrations in seven days, with a budget of two thousand rupees. The strategy starts with trusted campus networks. The target student wants a practical AI project they can explain in a placement interview. A clear project preview and a credible invitation should make registration more compelling. Every result shown here is simulated.

## 0:30 - 1:00 | 02 / THE GROWTH MODEL
The plan prioritizes three channels. Twenty active ambassadors reach forty approved, non-overlapping student groups. Four thousand eight hundred unique students, at fifteen percent visits and fifty percent registration conversion, produce three hundred sixty registrations. Optional peer referrals add one hundred twenty, and incremental club or placement email reach adds forty. The base case is five hundred twenty. These rates are assumptions to validate, and each email counts only once.

## 1:00 - 1:30 | 03 / STUDENT EXPERIENCE
The student flow collects fictional details and declared study year. It rejects non-final-year entries and duplicate emails, including capitalization changes. A successful registration receives a personal referral link. A second student opening that link is attributed to student referrals. The code must belong to an existing registration. This works in the same browser profile. It is a local prototype, so it does not verify identity or aggregate registrations across devices.

## 1:30 - 2:00 | 04 / CAMPAIGN DESK
The campaign desk turns the plan into something an operator can inspect. It shows source totals, sample versus manual records, remaining registrations and daily checkpoints. Day three targets one hundred eighty registrations; day six targets four hundred forty. The sensitivity calculator shows that reducing campus conversion from fifty to thirty percent lowers the base case to three hundred seventy-six. That shortfall calls for better proof and more approved reach, not optimistic reporting.

## 2:00 - 2:30 | 05 / BUDGET AND JUDGMENT
The two thousand rupee budget is capped: one thousand for actual ambassador expenses, five hundred for club logistics if needed, and five hundred as contingency. No money rewards raw signup counts. The invitation kit generates channel-specific links and editable-use drafts, but sends nothing. A conservative scenario produces only three hundred twenty registrations. The response is to measure the gap early, validate additional audience access, and report honestly if the target remains out of reach.

## 2:30 - 3:00 | 06 / AI AND LEARNING
The design evolved from a campus referral tracker into a measurable campaign system. AI helped create the implementation and planning materials. During review, the first video composition was rejected because its interface screenshot was unreadable. The localhost-only preview was also improved with a portable, single-file prototype. Another twenty-four hours would focus on student interviews, shared storage, email verification and volunteer testing. The learning notes document these actual revisions. This walkthrough uses synthetic narration, and no campaign was executed.
