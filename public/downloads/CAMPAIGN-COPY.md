## Test and measurement
- Randomly split comparable campus groups between A and B; avoid sending both variants to the same group. Seek at least 100 unique visits per variant before drawing directional conclusions; this is not guaranteed statistical significance.
- Measure unique landing visits, valid registrations and final-year eligibility. Use registrations / visits as the conversion metric. Use admin confirmation for reach estimates, clearly labelled as estimates.
- Store channel and ambassador code on first successful registration. If an email returns through a different link, preserve its original source; never award two registrations.
- 20 active ambassadors are a target, not an existing network. Start with 60 contact candidates across 20 colleges plus 10 reserve contacts; colleges and people must be researched before execution.
- Offer no cash for registration counts. Proposed reimbursements require proof of permitted activity and actual eligible expenses, with a fixed overall cap.
- End-of-campaign report: unique valid registrations, source split, cost, duplicate attempts, and assumptions that failed. Attendance is a separate downstream metric, not a substitute for registration.

## Why ambassadors would opt in
Offer a ready-to-share project preview, editable copy, a free workshop their batch can use, a clear time cap (one invitation and one reminder), and reimbursement of actual eligible expenses within the budget. A 20/60 acceptance rate is an assumption, not a secured network. Recruit through public club/placement contacts and classmates; obtain group-admin permission.

## Referral model consistency
Ask each willing sharer to invite two suitable friends. The base case uses 300 already-acquired campus/club registrants × 40% sharing × two incremental visits × 50% conversion = 120 registrations. Those two visits assume both invited friends open the link; validate this separately. Referrals do not seed themselves in the model.
